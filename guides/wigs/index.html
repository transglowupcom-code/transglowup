Wigs: Your First One, Start to Finish

A wig can change how you feel in an instant — and buying your first one can feel overwhelming. Here is everything you need, in order, from measuring your head to washing day.

November 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Why a wig, and why it is okay to be nervous

For many trans women and transfeminine people, a wig is one of the first big steps toward seeing yourself the way you want to be seen. It is also completely normal to feel nervous about it. You might worry about picking the wrong one, about it looking obvious, about what other people will think, or about spending money on something you are not sure will work. All of that is understandable, and none of it means you should not try.

A wig is not a test you can fail. It is a tool, like makeup or a favorite outfit, and the first one is for learning. You might love it, you might learn that you want a different length or color, and either outcome is useful. Start with something affordable and forgiving, practice wearing it at home, and let yourself get used to it before you decide anything. Your hair, your rules, your pace.

Measuring your head

Fit is everything, and fit starts with three simple measurements taken with a soft fabric measuring tape. You will want your hair flat for this, pinned down or braided, the way you plan to wear it under the wig, because the cap will sit over that. Measure twice and write the numbers down.

First, measure your circumference, the distance all the way around your head. Start at the center of your front hairline, bring the tape around one side just above your ear, around the fullest part of the back of your head and the nape of your neck, above the other ear, and back to where you started. Keep the tape flat and snug against your head, but do not pull it tight. This is the most important measurement, and it is the one most size charts are built around.

Second, measure front to nape: place the tape at your front hairline and run it in a straight line over the crown to the natural hairline at the nape of your neck. Third, measure ear to ear over the top: from the hairline above one ear, across the highest point of your head, down to the hairline above the other ear. These two extra numbers help fine-tune the fit, especially if you fall between sizes. If your measurements land in different size categories, most manufacturers advise choosing the size indicated by your largest measurement.

Cap sizes, in plain numbers

Wig sizing is not fully standardized across brands, so always check the size chart for the specific wig you are buying. That said, most brands use the same general bands. A petite or small cap usually fits a head circumference of about 20 to 21.5 inches (about 51 to 54.5 centimeters). An average or medium cap, the most common size, fits about 21.5 to 22.5 inches (about 54.5 to 57 centimeters). A large cap fits about 22.5 to 23.5 inches (about 57 to 59.5 centimeters). Some brands also offer extra small or extra large caps for measurements outside those bands.

Most wigs also include adjustable straps or elastic at the nape, which gives you a little extra give, and many caps have combs or clips inside to help them grip. If you have a lot of your own hair underneath, consider sizing up, since the volume of your natural hair fills the cap and makes the fit tighter. If you have very little hair underneath, you may find a snugger size more comfortable. Comfort matters more than the label: a cap that gives you a headache is too tight, and a cap that slides around is too loose, whatever the chart says.

Synthetic vs. human hair

The biggest choice you will make is the fiber. Synthetic wigs are made from man-made fibers, and they are the best starting point for most first-time buyers. They cost less, they are very low maintenance, and they hold their style through washing and weather, because the cut, wave, or curl was set during manufacturing and comes back after every wash. You can pull one out of the box, shake it out, and wear it. The trade-offs are that standard synthetic fiber cannot be heat styled, it can show some artificial shine, especially in very light or very dark shades, and it wears out sooner, typically lasting about four to six months with daily wear.

Human hair wigs are made from real hair, and they move, feel, and catch light like your own hair, especially in natural daylight. You can wash, blow-dry, curl, straighten, and even color them, and a professional can cut them. They also tend to last longer, often a year or more with daily wear and good care. The trade-offs are a higher cost, more upkeep, and the need to restyle them after washing, just like your own hair. They also react to humidity the way natural hair does.

There is also a middle option: heat-friendly synthetic wigs, made from fibers that tolerate low heat, so you can curl or straighten them gently. Always follow the brand's maximum temperature and use a heat protectant, and know that frequent heat styling can shorten the wig's life. For a first wig, a standard synthetic in a style close to what you want is usually the kindest choice: affordable, easy, and low pressure while you learn what you like.

Tip: Never use hot tools, a hot hair dryer, or hot water on a standard synthetic wig. Heat can permanently distort the fibers. If the label does not say heat-friendly, treat heat as off-limits — including ovens, barbecues, and leaving the wig in a hot car.

Cap types, briefly

The cap is the part that sits on your head, and different constructions feel and look different. A basic wefted cap, sometimes called open-cap, has rows of hair sewn onto strips with small gaps between them, which makes it lightweight and breathable. It is a good, affordable starting point. A monofilament top has a sheer section at the crown where each hair is individually tied, which looks more like a natural scalp and lets you part the hair in different directions. A lace front has a sheer lace panel along the hairline, which creates a more natural-looking hairline and lets you wear the hair pulled back from your face.

You do not need to understand every cap type before your first purchase. A basic cap is fine to learn with, and you can explore monofilament and lace front styles later once you know what matters to you. What matters most at the start is size, comfort, and a style you feel good wearing.

Putting one on

Putting on a wig is a skill, and it gets easier fast. Start with your own hair as flat as possible: braid it, pin it, or pull it into a low, flat style. A wig cap, a thin stretchy cap worn underneath, helps keep your hair in place, protects the wig from oils, and gives the wig something to grip. Put the wig cap on, tuck in any loose hairs, and smooth it down.

Hold the wig with both hands at the sides, tilt your head slightly forward, and place the front of the wig at your hairline. Then pull it back over your head, adjusting so the ear tabs sit evenly above your ears. Stand up straight, look in the mirror, and adjust the hairline until it looks natural. It should sit just at or slightly behind your natural hairline, not halfway down your forehead. Use the adjustable straps at the nape to snug it up, and tuck the combs inside if your cap has them.

Once it is on, style it gently with your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends and working upward. A little dry shampoo or wig spray can tame flyaways. Check the back with a hand mirror or a phone camera, since the back is the part you cannot see and the part other people will notice. Give yourself a few minutes to get used to the feeling. A new wig often feels strange for the first hour, and that feeling fades as you wear it.

Styling basics

For a synthetic wig, styling is mostly about gentle shaping, not heat. Use your fingers or a wide-tooth comb, and never brush a curly synthetic wig with a fine brush, which can frizz the fibers. If the wig looks too shiny when it is new, a light dusting of dry shampoo or a little wig powder can soften the sheen. If the bangs are too long, a hairdresser experienced with wigs can trim them; trimming a wig yourself is possible, but a first cut is worth doing professionally, since you cannot grow it back.

For a human hair wig, you can treat it much like your own hair: wash, blow-dry, curl, and straighten with heat protectant, and have it cut or colored by a professional. Keep styling products light, and avoid heavy oils and silicones that can build up on the cap. Whether synthetic or human, store your wig on a stand or a tall bottle when you are not wearing it, so it keeps its shape and does not tangle.

Washing and storing

Synthetic wigs need washing after about every six to eight wears, or every couple of weeks if you wear one daily. Human hair wigs can go longer, roughly every ten to fourteen wears. Always detangle gently before washing, using your fingers or a wide-tooth comb while the wig is still dry.

To wash a synthetic wig, fill a basin with cool water, never hot, and add a small amount of shampoo made for synthetic wigs. Submerge the wig and swish it gently for a minute or two. Do not rub, scrub, twist, or wring it. Rinse thoroughly in cool water until the water runs clear, then blot gently with a towel to remove excess water. Never squeeze or twist. Apply a leave-in conditioner made for synthetic hair if you like, then let the wig air-dry on a ventilated stand, away from heat, until it is completely dry. Do not comb or wear it while it is still wet.

Human hair wigs follow a similar routine but can be washed in cool or lukewarm water with a gentle shampoo, conditioned every time, and air-dried when you can. Heat tools are fine on human hair with a heat protectant. For storage, keep your wig on a stand in a cool, dry place, out of direct sunlight. A silk or satin bag can protect it during travel. With gentle care, your wig will last longer and look better for every wear.

Care note: Always follow the care label on your specific wig. Manufacturer guidance comes first, especially for heat limits and washing products, because fibers and cap constructions vary.

You are allowed to enjoy this

A wig is not about hiding. It is about choosing how you show up in the world, and that choice belongs to you. Your first wig might be perfect, or it might teach you what to look for in your second one. Either way, you are learning a skill, and you are doing something kind for yourself. Wear it around the house first, take a few photos, see how it feels, and go out when you are ready. There is no rush, and there is no wrong way to begin.
